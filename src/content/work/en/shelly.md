Your streak broke. Right on cue, the guilt kicks in. Shelly is a
routine-building app that refuses to work that way.

The client, the Daily Health Conference, is a non-profit promoting
wellness since 1983. The expertise is real; the technology lagged, and
memberships dropped with it. They needed a modern offer that keeps their
community, in a crowded health-tech space, strictly inside GDPR.

<p class="note">The research</p>

## The research

City life leaves no room for rigid wellness plans. Research with 16
participants showed the conflict:

<div class="stats">
  <div><span>15 of 16</span><span>want more physical activity</span></div>
  <div><span>10 of 16</span><span>cite mental health or stress as their top concern</span></div>
  <div><span>4 of 16</span><span>cite work as the main barrier to routine</span></div>
</div>

Existing apps punish the slip: miss a streak and the app judges you, so
people leave. And a majority prioritise mental wellbeing over physical
metrics.
The answer took its cue from the tortoise and the hare: slow, steady,
flexible habits. Hence the hare racing through the screens.

The six screens follow Maria: an agency job in Barcelona, irregular
hours, no appetite for another plan to fail at. Three sliders set the
routine, days, minutes and intensity. The loading screen stays in
character. Suggestions fit the day she is having, an exercise can be
paused or skipped without penalty, and progress counts strawberries
earned, not days survived. Around it, the identity from the research
wall: nature greens, warm yellows, rounded type. All sixteen
participants completed the core flow in the final usability round. In
2026 I redrew the same six screens by hand with text markers and set
them moving.

## Use of AI

The 2025 sprint was human work, with Anna, no AI in the loop: full UX
research and a full UI trajectory, sixteen participants, twenty-six
screens, from interviews, affinity mapping and personas through user
flows and usability rounds to a hi-fi prototype in Figma.

The screens above are the 2026 side. The Figma prototype already moved,
smart animate saw to that. I wanted to iterate it with code and improve
the design along the way. It started on paper again: the mood sketched
by hand, coloured fast with text markers, the turtle, the hare, the
cream pages of a field journal. One day of scribbling, those drawings
were the premise. Two days of vibe coding turned them into a working
product: the sliders drag, the loading screen changes its mind, the
dashboard leafs through week, month and year. Three directions came out
of those three days; this one won. I directed every choice, which lines wobble,
which ideas live, where the hare runs.

The research and the tested flow are untouched.
